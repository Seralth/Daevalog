# Architecture

Daevalog DPS Meter reads the AION 2 server-to-client game traffic, rebuilds each fight, and shows damage per player. Daevalog DPS Meter does not read game memory and does not change game files.

The backend is Rust (`src-tauri/src`). The front end is Qt Quick (`src-qml/`); it uses the backend (package `daevalog-dps-meter`, `src-tauri/Cargo.toml`) as the crate `daevalog` with the feature `backend`, without Tauri. The 1.x Tauri app (its desktop shell in `src-tauri/src/app/` and its JavaScript user interface in `public/src/js` and `index.html`) is not in this repository.

Daevalog is its own meter. It started as a fork of A2Tools DPS Meter v2.0.44 (https://github.com/taengu/A2Tools-DPS-Meter). Uploads tell a2tools.app the client `daevalog` and the meter's own version; saved fights, and fights rebuilt from a slice, keep `UPLOAD_COMPAT_VERSION` as the parser version (`src-tauri/src/version.rs`). Settings shows the release line of the front end (`src-qml/src/system.rs`) and the revision from `src-tauri/build.rs`.

## Layers

The data moves through six layers, in this order, and only forward. A layer reads from the layer before it and hands its result to the next one; the parser writes straight into fight storage. No layer reads from a later one.

| Layer | Job | Main files |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Capture | Read packets from the network card with libpcap. On Linux a helper process (`daevalog-capture`) holds the capture permission and pipes TCP payloads to the meter. Find the game connection and lock onto its port. | `src-tauri/capture-helper/` (crate `daevalog-capture`: libpcap, the helper, its pipe), `capture/live.rs`, `capture/helper_process.rs`, `capture/pcap_capturer.rs`, `capture/dispatcher.rs`, `capture/port_watch.rs` |
| 2. Stream | Join TCP payloads into one byte stream per connection. Once the port is locked, put the segments in order by TCP sequence number, so a segment the server sent again or that came out of order is read once, in its place (`TcpOrder`, in the dispatcher). The packet log is written after that step, so it holds the stream as the parser read it. | `capture/reassembly.rs`, `capture/byte_queue.rs`, `capture/tcp_order.rs` |
| 3. Framing | Cut the byte stream into game packets. Open compressed bundles. | `capture/framing.rs` |
| 4. Parsing | Read each game packet: damage, damage over time (DoT), heals, spawns, names, party roster, zone change, map load (which map a load enters, to tell an instance from the open world), combat state (`21 8D`: into or out of combat, and a boss's enrage deadline). The pet collection: every pet's level and souls (`00 90`, sent when the client joins a world server), souls added (`0D 90`), and a pet registered or levelled (`09 90`, `14 90`; `capture/decoder/pet_collection.rs`), queued in the storage for the pet soul notices. Power Shards: whether each of your own hits with a flags byte used one (bit 0x80), and a shard stack's count from the bag item update (`1B 56`; `capture/decoder/power_shards.rs`), queued for the shard notice. Damage taken: a monster skill's hit on a player (with its block, parry, endurance, regeneration, crit and front or back), a monster's DoT tick on a player, damage reflected onto a player, and an immune. A monster's skill-effect entity (spawn kind `0x1C`) names its monster at the end of its spawn record, so its hits count for that monster. Current HP: every `00 8D` record that holds it (monsters, you, your party, players near you; on every change) and the party record (`1B 92`, party members at any distance). | `capture/decoder/` (HP in `world.rs`), `entity/damage_packet.rs`, `entity/taken.rs` |
| 5. Storage | Keep the fight data: damage per target and per actor, names, summon owners, the local player. Healing and damage taken are kept hit by hit: a fight takes the hits taken during it by the people in it, per player and skill. A fight cleared before it is saved keeps its damage taken. Deaths of you and your party (rule 13) are kept one by one too, with when each player's HP was known: a fight takes the deaths from its first hit to 10 s after its last, with a count for you and each party member whose HP was known then. A fight cleared before it is saved keeps its deaths. | `combat/data_storage/` (damage taken in `taken.rs`, deaths in `deaths.rs`), `entity/owner_chain.rs` |
| 6. Calculation | Choose the targets for the meter mode, add up damage per player, compute fight time, save fights. Add up damage taken per player over the same fights (`DpsData::taken`), and per player and skill for Details and saved fights (`taken_skills`). Count deaths the same way (`DpsData::deaths`: you and each party member, `None` where the HP was not known; `deaths` in Details and saved fights, absent in fights saved before, which read as not known). Details shows several fights at once from the context's lists (`DetailBundle::taken_skills`, `DetailBundle::heal_skills`), where each hit taken and each heal tick counts once, and the fight's time as the meter counts it (`DetailBundle::battle_time`); in BOSS and TRAIN the context holds the meter's targets only (rule 14). Make the meter's rows by the 1.x page's rules, for the Qt meter (`MeterView`). Make what Details shows by the 1.x page's rules, from the same Details calls the page makes, for the Qt Details (`details_view`, `DpsCalculator::details_view`). | `combat/dps_calculator/`, `combat/meter_view.rs`, `combat/details_view.rs`, `history/fight_history.rs` |

The Qt meter reads layer 6 every 100 ms (`src-qml/src/live.rs`) and draws the rows `combat/meter_view.rs` makes. The Qt Details window reads layer 6 every 2 s while it is open, through a details reader (`DetailsSource`) as 1.x Details does, and draws what `combat/details_view.rs` makes (`src-qml/src/details.rs`). The Qt History window reads the saved fights and the game records as 1.x History does, and draws what `history/history_view.rs` makes (`src-qml/src/history.rs`). Each tick the live loop also takes the queued pet collection records and keeps each pet's count toward its next level (`src-qml/src/pet_souls.rs`); every soul updates that pet's one line in the notice tray (`src-qml/src/notices.rs`, where any part of the meter posts a notice, and a notice with the key of one already listed replaces it). It also takes the queued shard records: after your casts used Power Shards in the last ten minutes, three casts in a row without one post a single warning, "ran out" when the stack in use is empty, and a shard hit takes it away (`src-qml/src/power_shards.rs`). Every 15 s it also looks at the game's Field Boss and Spacetime Rift schedule (`src/data/world_spawns.json`, from the game's PeriodSpawn table) and posts the spawn notices that are due (`src-qml/src/world_spawns.rs`): the schedule is the server's clock, so the server time zone setting (Qt's time zone data) turns it into real times, and a notice's `until` makes the tray count down to the spawn.

## Windows

`src-qml/Main.qml` starts the meter window (`MeterWindow.qml`, or `LayerMeterWindow.qml` on a layer surface) and the tray, and opens the other windows when asked: `DetailsWindow.qml` (the live fight, or a saved one), `HistoryWindow.qml` and `SettingsWindow.qml`. The notice tray (`NoticeTray.qml`) is a popup window of its own on the meter's top edge: a slim bar with an icon per kind of notice waiting, pulled up into a list with its chevron. A notice with a time to count down to shows the minutes left where others show the time they came. Each window binds to the same QML objects (`src-qml/src/`), so a change shows in every open window at once.

## Data folder

Linux: `~/.local/share/daevalog-dps-meter/` (`daevalog-dps-meter-dev` for the developer build; `src-qml/src/settings_store.rs`). On its first start the release build takes over the settings and copies the saved fights of the 1.x folder, `~/.local/share/com.daevalog.dps-meter/`, which it leaves as it is (or moves the 2.0 test builds' folder there instead). Windows: `%APPDATA%\daevalog-dps-meter\`. The pet counts are kept there as `pet-souls.json`; counts from an earlier run are marked as possibly behind until the game sends the pet collection again. The developer build (the `dev` feature) also shows a red icon and "(dev)" in its tray tooltip and window title. The folder holds `settings.json`, `history/`, `slices/`, `debug.log`, `credentials.dat` once you sign in (it names the keyring item and holds no token) and, when packet logging is on, `packets_*.txt`. Packet logs contain chat and the names of other players. Do not share packet logs.

## Settings

`settings.json` is the only store for settings: one JSON object of strings. Every reader uses `config::settings::read_file`, and a file that does not read as such an object counts as empty.

- **Backend.** `Settings` (`config/settings.rs`) reads the file once and rewrites the whole file a moment after each change.
- **Front end.** The Qt `Settings` object (`src-qml/src/settings.rs`) holds every setting as a property, typed, with its default and checks in `src-qml/src/settings_store.rs`, which keeps the file through the backend's `Settings`. A change is checked and saved, the settings the backend acts on are handed to it, and every window binds to the one object.
- **Display Time.** Every time of day the windows show (History, the Details title, the game record view) goes through `history_view::Clock`: 12-hour or 24-hour as `dpsMeter.timeFormat` says, else as the system's clock says (`platform::process::clock_24h`: on Linux the time format of LC_ALL, else LC_TIME, else LANG; on Windows the Region settings), else 12-hour (`settings_store::clock_24h`). Fight lengths are durations and stay minutes and seconds; file names and the English bug-report copy keep 24-hour stamps.

## Rules that must hold

Each rule below fixed a real fault. Do not break a rule without a test that shows the new behaviour is correct against the game.

1. **Packet length.** The length varint of a packet equals the payload length after the varint, plus 4. This is true for every varint width and for bundles. See `framing::frame_size`.
2. **Upload slices are version 1.** The a2tools.app log service reads only version 1 slices. `fight_tape::encode` writes version 1 lengths (`downgrade_to_v1`). `decode` reads version 1 and version 2.
3. **Summon owners come from the game.** A summon gets its owner from the spawn packet (parent key or caster field), from an ownership record (`04 8D`) once it is a confirmed summon, or from a link record: skill code 1699xxxx from the summon to the owner, or 1677xxxx from the owner to the summon. The meter never guesses an owner from power scalar, and never from class alone: players share these values. The one fallback is an effect the game never spawned or linked (it sends none for other players' effects in a crowd): it goes to your party's one member of its class only when no other player of that class fought where it hit, else to one shared row (`entities::owners_in`).
4. **Link records are not damage.** A 1699xxxx or 1677xxxx record links a summon. The record never adds damage and never creates a target.
5. **A reused entity id starts clean.** A new spawn on an entity id removes the old owner link and the old marks of that id.
6. **Only the backend decides who the local player is.** The source is the game's self record. A window never sends an id to the backend. Settings sends only the character name the user types, which names the local player's entity but never picks it (`setting_changes::apply_character_name`). Party placeholder ids (90,000,000 and higher) are never the local player.
7. **No fight is lost.** Before any reset (zone change, party end, reset button, idle reset, exit), the meter saves every boss and training-dummy segment of 5 s or longer that you or your party fought.
8. **Each fight segment is saved once per change.** A segment is keyed by target id and start time. A second run on the same target is a new segment.
9. **Training dummies count every tick.** On a training dummy, DoT ticks after the actor's last direct hit count, as on any other target. The game's own meter counts them too: a scarecrow record can hold only DoT ticks, and each DoT row equals the ticks inside the record's window, moved 0.1 s earlier to fit the capture's times. The dummy list survives a reset, so a dummy fight after a reset is still saved (rule 7).
10. **Fight time is active time.** In modes with several targets, fight time is the union of the targets' active spans, not the longest span. Details on every target counts the same way: a boss and its adds fought at once count once, and the targets' spans are never added up. Its hit times count from the fight's first hit, not from each target's own. Details takes this time from the Details context (`DetailBundle::battle_time`), which counts it as the meter does: in TRAIN your own time on the dummies, so a stranger's hits on a dummy do not make Details longer than the meter.
11. **A spirit's ticks end with the spirit.** When a linked summon leaves the world (`42 36` flag 7), its DoT ticks after that do not count. The game's own meter does the same: its records of Malicious Whirlwind count every tick before the spirit left and none after.
12. **Settings have one store.** The front end reads and writes settings only in `settings.json`, never in a store of its own. In 1.x a value only the page's storage held showed in Settings without taking effect: on COSMIC the layer switch showed on while the meter ran as a normal window.
13. **A death is HP falling to 0, for you and your party only.** A player's current HP going from above 0 to 0 is one death; more readings of 0 are the same death. No player ever gets a `42 36` death record. Each time a player's HP reached 0 in real fights, it came with the game's death notice (`04 8D <victim> <skill> <killer>`) or, for a party member out of sight, the party record's last byte 0 (that byte is 0 at HP 0 and 1 above 0), and no player acted later than 0.2 s after it before a revive or a respawn. Deaths are counted for you and your party members only, while they are in the party; the party record reaches every member at any distance, so their count is complete, and a player whose HP the meter never had has no count, not 0. Nothing about deaths is uploaded.
14. **Details on every target is the meter's fight, in a fixed order.** The Details context lists the targets most damage first, then by id; in BOSS and TRAIN only the targets on the meter, with their healing, damage taken and deaths. It used to list every live target in a hash map's order, a new one on every read: the title, which named the first listed target, changed with each refresh, and BOSS mode's every-target view held whatever adds had not been dropped yet, so its healing was more than the meter's fight had. The title names the target the meter follows (`current_target_id`), else the one that took the most damage.
15. **A player's class is the class most of their hits are of** (`JobClass::by_hits`), in Details and in saved fights. A few skills are used by more than one class: a Templar, a Cleric and a Sorcerer were seen hitting with Lifestealing Blade (11340000, a Gladiator code), and the class of whichever skill came first in a hash map gave one player the Gladiator icon in one read and the Templar icon in the next. Details takes the class from the context, never from one skill row.

## Meter modes

| Mode | Targets | Fight time |
|---|---|---|
| TARGET | The enemy the local player or the local player's summons hit last. The one on screen stays while its last hit is no more than 3 s older than the newest. Nothing until the local player is known. | That target's span. |
| ALL | Every target, damage from every actor. Optional window: the last N minutes, or Off (since the zone change). | Union of active spans. |
| BOSS | The boss that the local player, the summons or the party hit hardest over the last 10 s. The boss on screen stays until another takes more than twice its damage from them, or it dies and they fight something newer. No boss: in the open world, their mob with the most damage. A zone change after the local player or the party hit a boss (a revive after a wipe) does not clear the screen: the fight stays, Details and the Heal and Tank tabs with it, read from a copy of the storage taken just before the change, until the local player or the party hit a boss again, the mode changes, or the meter is cleared, so the party can go over the wipe (trash on the way back is TARGET's). Saving reads the live storage. | That boss's span. |
| TRAIN | Training dummies that the local player or the summons hit. | Union of the local player's spans on those dummies. |
| ENC | The enemies of the current encounter. A hit by the local player or a party member (their summons count as them), or an enemy's hit on one of them, starts an encounter, and that enemy joins it. Other players' hits count only on enemies already in it. Until the meter knows the local player, every nearby fight counts. The encounter ends after a quiet time with no combat by the local player or the party: 15 s by default, 5 to 300 s in Settings. A living boss keeps it open for up to 5 minutes of quiet. | From the encounter's first hit to its last hit. |
